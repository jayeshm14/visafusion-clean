Q1:

What are Udaan's fee components?

Ans:

The cost of UIPL services is comprised of four major components: government/document issuance fees (attestation), UIPL base service fees, rush surcharges and pick up & delivery charges. Government/document issuance outlays refer to legalization fees or authentication fee. UIPL service fees refer to the professional service fee incurred for each document-processing request. The amount of the service fee depends on the individual rate agreement your company has negotiated with UIPL. Rush surcharges can be incurred depending on the amount of time given to UIPL to process your document. Delivery fees will vary depending on the particular shipping arrangements you have made with UIPL and on the amount of time given between the processing of your document and your traveler's departure date.

Q2:

When does UIPL apply "service" and "rush" fee surcharges for visa processing?

Ans:

The "service" surcharge applies when UIPL is given 4 to 7 business days for processing after receipt of complete application materials. The "rush" surcharge applies when UIPL is given 3 days or less for processing after receipt of complete application materials.

Q4:

What is consular jurisdiction, and how does it affect my visa processing?

Ans:

Consular jurisdiction is the geographic area for which a foreign mission has responsibility for issuing visas. Some foreign missions have strict enforcement of jurisdiction, some only enforce it for certain types of visas and others do not enforce jurisdiction or have any jurisdictional limits at all. Depending on what jurisdictional limits exist, UIPL can sometimes direct you to another city for faster or less complicated processing.

Q5:

What is the difference between visa validity vs stay duration?

Ans:

Validity refers to the amount of time before a travel document expires. Stay Duration refers to the maximum stay in a country that is permitted on a visa. A Brazilian multiple entry visa may have a validity of five years, however, the maximum duration of stay at any one time is 90 days.

Q6:

What is the difference between a Stay Visa and an Entry Visa?

Ans:

A Stay Visa is one, where the pax is allowed to enter and exit the concerned country, within the given issue date and the expiry date mentioned on the visa whereas an Entry Visa allows the pax to enter the concerned country even on the expiry date mentioned on the visa and can be there till the stay duration granted or mentioned on the visa. Please note that generally all Schengen visas are stay visas whereas US, Canada, Singapore, etc are entry visas. What is a visa invitation?

Ans:

: Visa invitations are documents approved by the Foreign Ministry of the country of travel that authorize a particular traveler to visit their country. Most often, they indicate specific information regarding the itinerary (date & duration of stay, name of hosting organization, cities of visit), as well as an official reference number and seal of approval (if applicable only). Q11:

Can you guarantee that the foreign mission, I have sent documentation to will grant my visa?

Ans:

No, UIPL cannot guarantee this. For a variety of reasons, any foreign mission can reject your application request at any time. The most common reasons that a visa would be rejected are: using pencil or red or green ink, incomplete forms, business cover letters that do not contain all of the required information, non-passport quality photos, passport not signed, passport with lesser validity, using white out, using copied or traced signatures, invitation not properly filled out, and attaching copies when originals are required, passports which has visa rejection, previous over-stay, among others.

Q12:

Our office keeps photocopies of visa applications on hand. Will these cause problems in processing?

Ans:

UIPL strongly discourages keeping photocopied visa applications on hand. Application forms change frequently, and old applications are often not accepted. In addition, an application accepted by the foreign mission in one city might not be the same application accepted by the foreign mission in another city. Also, some foreign mission will accept copies and some will only accept originals. We recommend you contact UIPL for updated requirements whenever a visa is required.

Q13:

I live in New Delhi. My co-worker in Mumbai/ other city got the exact same visa I did but she wasn't required to provide as much documentation. Why?

Ans:

Just as requirements vary between different countries for visas, requirements can also vary from city to city for the same country. United Kingdom is a very good example of this. What the United Kingdom Embassy needs in Delhi may or may not be the same for what the consulate needs in Chennai.

Q15:

What does it mean when a "personal appearance" is required to get a visa?

Ans:

Some consulates require applicants to appear in person at the consulate when applying for a visa. This may be done just to verify the applicant's identity or a formal interview may be conducted. In these cases, third parties (like UIPL) are not permitted to facilitate visa paperwork. However, some consulates that require personal appearances will allow third parties to pick up documents on behalf of the traveler once documents are processed. Contact UIPL's Communication Team.

Q16:

Why must I have a full blank page in my passport when I need to get a visa? Why can't a visa be issued on a blank second last page at the back of my passport?

Ans:

Most visas are stamped or affixed in your passport on the blank pages specifically marked for visas, and all countries require that visa stamp be placed on those pages that do not contain any other stamps.

Q17:

Does UIPL process visa requests for any nationality? What is the normal processing time for a visa for a non-Indian- citizen?

Ans:

UIPL handles visa requests for all nationalities whose visa as per the jurisdiction rules can be processed in India. The turn-around time depends on a number of factors and criteria. Indian nationals visas are considered easier to process by many consulates, while others can be more challenging. For example, Indian nationals who require a visa for entry to United Kingdom are easier to process than someone who holds a Srilankan/ Pakistani passport. Many foreign missions must obtain approval from their home government prior to issuing a visa for citizens of certain countries. If a referral to the home government is required, processing can take considerably longer.

Ans:

Q20:

What type of a visa do I need - business, tourist, transit or any other visa?

Ans:

A business visa is a visa issued to travelers who intend to visit a country for the purpose of commercial meetings, discussions, negotiations, and similar activities for a limited period of time. Business visas are usually insufficient for travelers who will undertake work or temporary work assignments in a country. Some countries will require special types of business visas depending on the type of business you are conducting, such as for technical work, training sessions or speaking engagements. A tourist visa is a visa issued to travelers who intend to visit a country for the purpose of tourism or to visit family and friends for a limited period of time. A transit visa is issued to travelers who must travel through certain countries in order to get to their ultimate destination. Staying on the airplane or remaining in the airport will not preclude you from having to get a transit visa. Most visas can be issued quickly and can prevent a number of travel related inconveniences. If in doubt about the type of visa needed, contact UIPL's Communication Team.

Q21:

What is a Schengen visa?

Ans:

The Schengen visa gets its name from a small town in Luxembourg where a treaty was signed in 1995 to end internal border checkpoints and controls. The following European countries currently honor this visa: Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Iceland, Italy, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, and Sweden. With a Schengen visa, you may enter one country and travel freely throughout the Schengen zone. It applies only to nationalities who would normally be required to obtain a visa prior to traveling to those countries. (For example, this would typically NOT apply to U.S. citizens.) Travelers should apply for a visa for the country in which he/she intends to stay the longest (the main destination). If the traveler does not have a main destination, he/she should apply for a visa for the country, which is the first point of entry.

Q23:

What are the differences between multiple, double or single entry visas?

Ans:

A multiply entry** visa allows the traveler to visit a country an unlimited number of times as long as the visa remains valid and the duration of stay is within the limits. A double entry visa allows two entries, and single entry visa allows the holder to visit a country only once before needing a new visa. ** Conditions apply
